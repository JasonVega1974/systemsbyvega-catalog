The pile is not a character flaw

Most people who call are good at their actual job and quietly buried under everything around it — the return that never gets made, the important email sitting three weeks down the inbox, the appointment that needed rescheduling twice. None of it is difficult. It is just endless, and it does not fit inside a day that already has a job in it. This is a service for taking the endless part, writing it down, and handing it back finished.

The rhythm

How a week actually works

  1. Monday

    The list gets set

    We start the week on the same short call. You talk, I write, and before lunch you have the week's list back in writing — what I am handling, and what still needs a decision from you.

  2. Tuesday

    Desk day

    Inbox triage, calendar clean-up, appointments booked and confirmed, quotes chased. The quiet work that never gets done when you are the one who has to do it.

  3. Wednesday

    Out and about

    One run through town covers the returns, the shipping, the pharmacy and the pickups, ordered so the driving makes sense rather than the list order.

  4. Thursday

    Loose ends

    The things that needed a callback, a second attempt, or someone else to reply first. Most of them close on a Thursday, and the ones that do not get flagged.

  5. Friday

    Handoff

    A short written summary: what happened, what is still open, and what has already been carried onto next week's list. Then the weekend is yours.

That is the usual shape of it. Retainer clients set their own rhythm — some want a check-in every morning, some would rather not hear from me until Friday.

Getting going

How we start

A short call

Free, and no pressure attached. You describe the pile, I ask a lot of questions, and I tell you honestly whether this is worth paying for in your situation.

The handover

We sit down once, in person or on a screen share, and I take notes on how you like things done — who to call, what to never touch, and how you want to be asked.

A first block

We start with a half-day so you can watch the work happen before committing to anything longer. The written summary at the end of it is yours to keep either way.

Then a rhythm

Hourly when you need it, a standing block each week, or a monthly retainer. You can move between them at the end of any month, without a conversation about it.

Rates

Three ways to buy the time

Rates cover time on the clock. Purchases, postage, parking and anything else bought on your behalf are passed through at cost with receipts attached to the summary — no markup, no handling fee. Unused retainer hours do not roll over, and I will say so before you buy the retainer rather than after.

The edges

Where I stop

A service like this only works if the edges are clear. These are the things I do not do — not because they are beneath the job, but because they belong to someone qualified, or to you.

I do not give professional advice

No legal, tax, medical or financial guidance. I can book the appointment, gather the paperwork and sit in to take notes. The call itself is not mine to make.

I do not take custody of your money

No bank access, no stored card numbers, no signing authority. Purchases go on a card you control, or I front small amounts and invoice them back at cost with receipts.

I do not do care work

I am not a caregiver, a nurse or a nanny. No medications, no personal care, and no supervising children or adults who need supervision. Those are different trades with different training behind them.

I do not do heavy labour or cleaning

No deep cleaning, no moving furniture, no yard work, no hauling. I will find the person who does it, book them, meet them at the door and check the work.

I do not sign in your name

Contracts, forms and anything binding get prepared, explained and put in front of you. The signature stays yours.

I do not disappear into your systems

Access is granted per tool, in your name, and you can pull any of it at any time. You keep the passwords. I keep a written record of what I touched and when.

If something on your list sits on one of these lines, say so on the first call. It is usually still solvable — just not by me alone.
