Most windows are half-cleaned, and everybody can tell

The outside gets a hose and the inside gets a paper towel, and the glass still hazes the second the sun hits it. We treat a window as four surfaces, not one: the exterior pane, the interior pane, the screen that has been filtering road dust all summer, and the track full of grit that dirties both again the next time you slide the sash. Do all four in one visit and the light comes through the way the builder intended.

How it works

From pane count to low-sun walk-through

Count and quote

Tell us roughly how many panes you have, or send a couple of photos. We give you a flat number off the tier list before anyone shows up, and it does not move on the day.

Set up clean

Drop cloths under every interior sill, ladder standoffs so nothing rests on your gutters or siding, and shoes off or booties on for the inside pass.

Wash, squeegee, detail

Soap and scrub the pane, pull the squeegee in overlapping passes, then hand-dry the edges and corners with a dedicated cloth. Screens come out, get washed, and go back in the opening they came from.

Walk it with you

We walk the house with you at low sun, which is when streaks show. Anything you point at gets redone right then. You pay when you are happy with the glass, not before.

What's included

One visit, four surfaces

Every item below is inside the flat price. Nothing here is an upsell we spring on you at the door.

Exterior glass

Washed from ground level, water-fed pole or ladder, whichever the elevation calls for. No pressure washer anywhere near a seal.

Interior glass

Cloths and drop sheets in every room, blinds lifted and returned to where you had them, no drips on the sill.

Screens

Pulled, washed on both faces, dried and re-set in the same opening — screens are numbered as they come out so nothing ends up in the wrong window.

Tracks and sills

Vacuumed first, then wiped out wet and dried. This is the step most quotes skip, and it is the one that keeps the glass clean longer.

Frames and mullions

Hand-detailed so the white vinyl actually reads white, and the grid bars do not leave a dusty shadow across the pane.

Hard-water and paint spots

Mineral etching from a sprinkler and construction overspray both come off with a separate process. We flag them at the quote so the price is honest before we begin.

Flat pricing

Priced by the pane, not by the hour

Roughly how many panes are we cleaning?

Count a pane as one piece of glass you can see through. A double-hung window is two. A slider is two. A french-grid sash counts as one, not nine — the grid bars are frame, not glass.

Every figure above is a flat rate for the whole visit, inside and out, screens and tracks included. Storey height, hard-water treatment and construction cleanup are quoted separately and always in writing first.

Straight answers

The questions we get on the doorstep

Do I need to be home?+
For an exterior-only visit, no — leave the gate unlocked and we will text you when we are done. For an inside-and-out visit somebody needs to let us in, and we would rather you were there at the end for the walk-through anyway.
How high will you go?+
Second-story exteriors are routine, from a water-fed pole where the ground allows and a ladder with standoffs where it does not. Third storey and anything over a roofline gets looked at in person before we quote it.
Will you get the hard-water spots off?+
Usually. Sprinkler overspray leaves mineral deposits that a normal wash will not touch, so they get a separate restoration pass. If the glass is etched past the point where that works, we will tell you rather than charge you to find out.
What about the weather?+
We work through most of the North Idaho year. Below freezing the water sheets and re-freezes on the glass, so winter jobs get rescheduled to the next workable day at no charge — you never lose a deposit, because we do not take one.
How often should this happen?+
Twice a year suits most homes here — once after the spring pollen drops and once before the holidays. Storefronts on a dusty street usually want a monthly exterior route with a full inside pass a few times a year.

Meet the owner

The person on the ladder
