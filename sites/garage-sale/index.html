Nobody actually wants to run a garage sale

The pricing decisions, the 6 a.m. start, the haggling, the pile of unsold stuff at 4 p.m. — that's the part people dread, and that's the part we take. We run the sale on your driveway, on your behalf: your things stay yours until a shopper pays for them, and everything ends with an itemized settlement sheet and an empty garage. One thing we'll never do is guess out loud what your sale will bring — nobody honestly can. What we promise is the work.

How it works

From cluttered to swept in one weekend

Sort & stage

We walk the garage with you and flag anything the family keeps. Then the crew sorts the rest into categories and stages the driveway — everything up on tables, best items nearest the street.

Price & sign

Every category gets priced to actually sell, small items get bundled, and our corner signs go up around the neighborhood the night before — big letters, driver height.

Sale day, staffed

We work the sale from early birds to the last hour: greeting, haggling, cash and card checkout. You can watch from the kitchen window or leave town entirely.

Leftovers hauled or donated

Whatever hasn't sold by close gets donated or hauled away by arrangement. You get the garage back swept, with an itemized settlement sheet of everything that sold.

Prep tips

How a good sale day gets set up

Six things our crew does on every driveway — worth stealing even if you'd rather run your own.

Everything off the ground

Items on the floor don't sell. We bring folding tables and raise everything to waist height — even a sheet of plywood on sawhorses outsells a tarp on the lawn.

Best things face the street

A driver decides in two seconds whether to stop. The furniture, tools and big pieces go nearest the curb, where they do the advertising for everything behind them.

Make a walkway, not a wall

Tables form a corridor shoppers walk through, so they end up seeing everything — a single line of tables across the driveway is a barricade, not a sale.

Signs get four words

A driver thirty feet away has time for about four big words and an arrow. Our signs go up on the corners the night before, at eye height for a driver — not knee height on a fence.

Price by category, then bundle

We sort first and price whole categories at once instead of agonizing item by item. Small stuff gets bundled — 'fill a bag' moves volume and saves hours of tagging.

A markdown plan empties the garage

The point of the last day is a clear garage, not a full one. We set the markdown schedule with you in advance, so the sale ends on purpose instead of fizzling out.
