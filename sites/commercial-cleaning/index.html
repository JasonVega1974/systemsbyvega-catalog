How it starts

From a walkthrough to a signed scope

Walk the building

We meet you on site — after hours if that is easier — and measure the cleanable square footage room by room. Restroom count, floor types and trash volume all get written down.

Written scope and price

You get a task list with a frequency against each line and a price against the whole thing. Nothing is ordered, scheduled or charged until you have read it.

Crew assigned by name

The people who will be in your building are named on the contract, issued their own access, and walked through the scope with you before the first shift.

Sign-off, every visit

A dated sheet stays in the building. Anything missed gets written on it, and we are back before the next scheduled visit to close it out.

      Before you ask us

      The questions managers actually ask

      Do you clean during business hours?+
      Rarely. Most buildings on our route are done after close, between six and midnight, so nobody has to work around a mop bucket. Day-porter coverage during business hours can be added to any contract if your lobby needs it.
      Who has keys and codes to my building?+
      Named people, and only named people. Everyone assigned to your building is listed on the contract, and that list changes in writing, not quietly. Keys and alarm codes are signed in and out each shift.
      What happens when something gets missed?+
      Write it on the sign-off sheet or send us a message, and we are back before the next scheduled visit at no charge. The sheet tells us who was in the building and what they covered, so we can usually answer what happened the same day.
      Do you bring your own equipment and supplies?+
      Yes — machines, chemicals and cloths are ours and stay on our truck. Consumables your building hands to the public, like liners, paper and soap, are itemized at cost, or you can keep buying your own if that is cheaper for you.
      How long is a contract, and how do I get out of it?+
      Month to month after the first ninety days, with thirty days notice either way. We would rather keep a building because the work holds up than because a term said we get to.
