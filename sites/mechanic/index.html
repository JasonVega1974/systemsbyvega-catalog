Why mobile

A shop bay is mostly just a floor with a roof over it

Most of what a vehicle asks for in a normal year — pads, rotors, a battery, a belt, fluids, a scan tool plugged into the port under the dash — does not need a lift, a lobby, or your car sleeping in somebody else's lot for three days. It needs a mechanic, a jack, a torque wrench and a flat patch of ground. That is the whole idea here: the truck comes to the vehicle, and you keep your keys and your day.

  • Nothing gets towed and nothing waits in a queue behind somebody else's transmission.
  • Watch the whole job over our shoulder if you want to — the old part goes in your hand or your bin, your call.
  • The flat rate arrives by text before we load the truck, and it does not move unless you approve a change.
  • Work parking lots count. Plenty of jobs get finished between clock-in and lunch.

What travels in the truck

The jobs that do not need a lift

Everything below happens at your address. Anything that genuinely needs a rack, a booth or a hoist gets named as such and sent somewhere with one.

Brakes at the curb

Pads, rotors and hardware, torqued to spec on your own driveway. The squeal you have been ignoring is an afternoon at home, not a week without your vehicle.

Batteries & charging

We test before we sell: battery health, alternator output, and the parasitic drain that quietly kills a good battery in a month. Then we replace what actually failed.

Oil, filters & fluids

Full synthetic or conventional, a new filter, and every level checked while the hood is up. The mat goes down first and the old oil leaves with us.

Starters, alternators & belts

The bolt-on failures that strand people at the worst moment. Most of them come off and go back on right where the vehicle already sits.

Scan-tool diagnostics

Check-engine lights, misfires, mystery drains. We plug in, road test with you riding along, and tell you the actual fault instead of the most expensive possibility.

Workplace & multi-vehicle visits

Several vehicles at one address is one trip and one arrival window. Ask about a route stop for a whole work lot instead of booking them one at a time.

Straight answers

Questions people ask before they book

What do you need at my house?+
A flat, reasonably level place to work beside the vehicle — a driveway, a carport pad, a parking space. We bring the tools, the jack, the stands, the lighting and a mat for the ground. Power and water are ours too.
What if it turns out to need a real shop?+
Then we say so. Anything wanting a lift, an alignment rack, a paint booth or a transmission on a jack is not a driveway job, and pretending otherwise would cost you twice. You get told what it needs and where it should go.
Do you work in apartment lots and business lots?+
Usually, with the property manager's blessing. Send the address first and we will tell you honestly whether the spot works before you go asking anyone.

Meet the mechanic

The guy in the driveway

Silas Renner

Silas Renner learned the trade the way a lot of small-town mechanics do: fixing whatever showed up in the yard, because the nearest shop was forty minutes and a tow bill away. Gravel & Gasket started as a toolbox in the back of a pickup and a habit of saying yes to neighbors, and it still runs on the same rule — if it can be done properly where it sits, it gets done where it sits, and if it cannot, you get told that plainly instead of expensively.
